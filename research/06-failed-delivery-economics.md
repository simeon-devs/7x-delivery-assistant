# Failed delivery economics and carrier performance

Research run 21 Sep 2026. Labels: **(a) VENDOR MARKETING** · **(b) JOURNALISM** ·
**(c) COMPANY-REPORTED** · **(d) INDEPENDENT / REGULATOR / ACADEMIC**.

---

## 1. THE NUMBER NOT TO USE

**"$17.78 per failed delivery" is Loqate vendor marketing from November 2020.**
Press release dateline: NEW YORK, 12 Nov 2020. Verbatim: *"cost the retailer an average of
**$17.78 per failed order**"* and *"**one in 20 online orders never arriving** at their intended
destinations"*.
Stated methodology: *"Loqate's 2020 holiday survey of **1,200 American consumers**."*

**The release never states how $17.78 was derived. A consumer survey of 1,200 people cannot
produce a retailer cost-per-order.** This is the origin of the most-reposted "cost of a failed
delivery" figure on the internet. The variant "$17.20" traces to Locus's own internal cost
framework, and **Locus itself says these are "benchmarks for validation only, not
operation-specific answers"**.

**A value case built on $17.78 collapses under one question.** Label it as a vendor estimate of
unstated construction, or do not use it.

### Two more that circulate and do not hold
- **"Last mile is 53% of total shipping cost."** UNVERIFIED, usually attributed to Business
  Insider, primary source not found. Routinely confused with Capgemini's 41%, which is a
  different construct (share of **supply chain** cost, not **shipping** cost). Never
  interchangeable.
- **"1 in 10 parcels fail first-time delivery."** UNVERIFIED as a market rate. It is a
  misreading of Loqate's finding that **24% of businesses** see more than 1 in 10 orders fail at
  first attempt, which is a distribution statement about firms, not a failure rate.

## 2. WHAT TO USE INSTEAD

### 2.1 Capgemini, "The last-mile delivery challenge" (2019) — (d), with a caveat
Fieldwork Oct-Nov 2018. **N=2,874 consumers in 5 countries; N=500 supply-chain executives in 9
countries.** Primary PDF is live and retrievable:
https://www.capgemini.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Report-Digital-%E2%80%93-Last-Mile-Delivery-Challenge1.pdf

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| Average cost of a last-mile delivery **to the organisation** | **USD 10.10** |
| Average delivery cost **charged to the consumer** | **USD 8.08** |
| Average consumers are **willing to pay** | **USD 1.40** |
| Consumers willing to pay the full delivery cost | **1%** |
| Last-mile share of total supply chain cost | 41% (Fig. 13) |

**The economics gap is the argument, not the 41%.** It costs $10.10, the customer is charged
$8.08, and the customer thinks it is worth $1.40. Every delivery already loses money before a
single attempt fails.
**Caveats to state: grocery-weighted, and the 41% is executives' self-reported cost allocation
from a 500-person survey, not audited carrier cost accounting.**

### 2.2 IMRG + NetDespatch — the best real first-attempt failure trend — (b) reporting (d)
Via ChannelX, Chris Dawson, 17 Apr 2019. IMRG's underlying report is members-only.
- **Failed delivery attempt rate: 4.65% (2014) → 2.77%** (12-month rolling average, Mar 2018 to
  Feb 2019). **This comes from IMRG's continuous Delivery Index measurement**, so it is the
  better-defended number.
- Next-day / specified-day share of UK volume: 43.9% (Jul 2016) → 50.2% (Jul 2018).
- The **£1.6bn/year UK cost of failed delivery** figure is from the same work but its methodology
  was never disclosed and the primary document is gated. If pressed, concede it is a trade-body
  estimate of undisclosed construction.

### 2.3 Ofcom — the prevalence number — (d) REGULATOR
Published 22 Oct 2025. Yonder Consulting, **n=4,058 UK adults 16+**, surveyed Jan and Jul 2025,
weighted on age, gender, region, ethnicity and social grade.
- **68% of parcel recipients experienced a delivery issue in the past six months**
- Delays 28%, parcel left somewhere inappropriate 26%, driver not knocking loudly enough 20%,
  not enough time to answer the door 19%
- Disabled consumers: **73% vs 65%**
- Contact-handling satisfaction **41% (2023) → 45% (2025)**; complaints handling 43% → 46%
- Overall satisfaction 78%, unchanged. Customer service: Amazon and FedEx 57%, UPS and DHL 55%,
  Yodel 38%, **Evri 31%**
- **UK sent and received a record 4.2 billion parcels**, +7% YoY

## 3. THE SINGLE STRONGEST NUMBER IN THIS PACK

**Royal Mail, Delivery Routes Completed Daily: 77.42% against a 99.90% target in FY2025/26,
down from 87.80% the year before.**

Company-reported under DUSP Condition 1.10, measured by Spectos, methodology and results verified
by Ofcom-appointed auditors. Primary PDF:
https://www.internationaldistributionservices.com/media/13039/year-end-adjusted-quality-of-service-report-2025-26.pdf

**Roughly one delivery route in four was not completed on a given day.** That is not a "failed
attempt" in the courier sense (nobody home). It means **the round was never walked**. It is the
closest thing in any public disclosure to a structural non-delivery rate.

### Royal Mail FY2025/26 full table (all excluding the defined Christmas period)
| Standard | Target | Unadjusted | MBORC-adjusted |
|---|---|---|---|
| USO 1st Class (D+1) | 93.0 | **75.7** | 76.1 |
| USO 2nd Class (D+3) | 98.5 | **90.2** | 90.3 |
| Special Delivery (D+1 by 1pm) | 99.0 | 95.4 | — |
| Delivered Postcode Area Target | 91.5 | **0 of 118 areas** | 0 of 118 |
| **Delivery Routes Completed Daily** | **99.90** | **77.42** | — |
| Items Deemed Correctly Delivered | 99.50 | 99.76 | — |

FY2024/25 comparator: 1st Class 76.5, 2nd Class 92.2, **Delivery Routes Completed Daily 87.80**.

### Regulatory consequence
Ofcom fined Royal Mail **£21m on 15 Oct 2025** for 2024/25 (reduced 30% from £30m for admission
of liability; third-largest fine Ofcom has ever imposed; third consecutive breach year). Prior:
£10.5m (Dec 2024), £5.6m (Nov 2023). **Cumulative fines for delivery failures now exceed £37m.**
Ofcom opened a further investigation on 1 Jun 2026 into 2025/26.
**Targets were lowered from 1 April 2026 to 90% First Class and 95% Second Class**, so any
comparison across that boundary is a target change, not a performance change.

## 4. THE COMPLAINT MIX, AND WHO ACTUALLY PAYS

Royal Mail, Q4 2025/26, mandated disclosure under Ofcom Consumer Protection Condition 3.3.16.
Scope: one quarter, USO/regulated products, non-contract customers.

| Category | Complaints completed | Recompense paid |
|---|---|---|
| Loss | 48,844 | £929,272 |
| Misdelivery | 27,975 | £1,697 |
| USO Delivery Failure | 27,555 | £802 |
| Delay | 18,627 | £241,402 |
| Redirection | 12,668 | £46,261 |
| **Redelivery Failure** | **12,774** | **£1,563** |
| **P739 Failure** (the "Something for you" card) | **6,744** | **£322** |
| Delivery Procedure Errors | 6,622 | £735 |
| **Grand Total** | **214,111** | **£1,544,825** |

**Redelivery Failure + P739 Failure = 19,518 complaints in a single quarter, with combined
recompense of £1,885.**

That gap is the argument: **the cost of a failed redelivery sits with the operator and the
customer, not in compensation.** Nobody is paid out. The money leaves as vans, drivers, wasted
stops and churn.

## 5. Other operators, and the traps in comparing them

**Australia Post FY25** (ANAO-audited, measured by Kantar): **98.70% of letters delivered early
or on time** against a 94% target; 99.4% on time or within one working day.
**TRAP: this is a LETTERS standard against a more forgiving delivery-time undertaking. It is not
comparable to Royal Mail's 75.7% next-day figure.**
Parcels: ~103 million delivered in peak (1 Nov-31 Dec 2024), +3.1%, "a record high rate of
Delivery In Full On Time" — **but the DIFOT percentage itself is not disclosed.**

**PostNL Annual Report 2025** (Mail measured by an independent firm and reported to ACM):
- Parcels NL delivery quality **97%** (2024 and 2025)
- Mail NL next-day **86%** preliminary; within 2 days 95.3%
- Parcel volume 376 million, +1.2%
- **TRAP 1: the parcels figure is "normalised for regular weeks, and therefore excludes peak
  weeks... resulting in around 75% of weeks included."** A 97% that excludes Christmas is not the
  same construct as Royal Mail's or Ofcom's.
- **TRAP 2: PostNL's narrative also says "domestic parcels achieved 99% on-time performance".
  That sentence is about BELGIUM, not the Netherlands. Do not quote 99% as a Dutch or group
  figure.**

**Deutsche Post DHL: NOT OBTAINED.** Every DHL host timed out over both HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/2, IPv4
and IPv6. No DHL figure is reported here rather than quoting one from memory.

**METRIC WARNING covering this whole section:** every headline above is an **on-time / speed**
metric (D+1, D+3, delivered-by-time). **None of them is a first-attempt-success rate.** The only
two Royal Mail lines that approach failure-to-deliver are Delivery Routes Completed Daily and
Items Deemed Correctly Delivered.

## 6. Academic position

Song, Cherrett, McLeod & Guan (2009), *Transportation Research Record* 2097, DOI 10.3141/2097-02:
collection and delivery points work best *"when the proportion of **first-time home delivery
failures is greater than 20%**"*, with customer travel cost reductions up to 90%.
**That >20% is a modelled break-even threshold, NOT a measured failure rate. Do not cite it as
"20% of deliveries fail."**

**General finding:** Crossref and Semantic Scholar queries on first-attempt delivery failure rates
return almost nothing empirical. Peer-reviewed last-mile papers overwhelmingly cite the same
vendor and trade-body numbers (Capgemini, IMRG) rather than independent measurement.
**There is no independent academic measurement of parcel first-attempt failure rates.**
That absence is itself a defensible thing to say in a room.

## 7. THE DEFINITIONAL CATCH ON OUR OWN DATASET

Our 858 real rows show 142 in a failed-delivery state, about **16.6% overall**, and by emirate
Ajman 32.1%, Sharjah 25.0%, Dubai 23.3%, Abu Dhabi 21.2%, RAK 11.9%, Fujairah 10.0%.

**IMRG measured the UK first-attempt failure rate at 2.77%.** Ours looks 6 to 12 times worse.

Before using that as a finding, note the definitions are not the same thing:
- IMRG measures **failed attempts as a share of all delivery attempts**, continuously.
- Our number is **a status snapshot** — the share of shipments *currently sitting in* a failed
  state, which over-weights shipments that failed and stayed failed, and under-weights those that
  failed once and then succeeded.

**Honest handling: present the emirate spread as a relative signal (Ajman is 3x Fujairah, and
that is real within this dataset), and explicitly decline to compare the absolute rate to IMRG's
2.77% because the denominators differ.** Declining that comparison, out loud, is worth more than
making it.
